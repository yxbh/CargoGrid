# Geometry reference

The [README](../README.md) covers everyday use. This page is the deeper reference for dimensions, interfaces, print poses and what the checks do.

## Units and datums

All dimensions are millimetres. The southwest corner of the tile body is the XY datum, and Z=0 is the underside. In the Python API and manifest, `Interface.pitch` is the user-facing unit size and `Interface.height` is tile thickness. X-socket centres are at `(pitch/2 + i*pitch, pitch/2 + j*pitch)`.

Let `u` be unit size and `t` be tile thickness. The main tile body is `u*nx` by `u*ny` by `t`. Male tabs on the east and north edges project `0.1*u`, south pockets are `0.1*u` deep and west pockets are 0.1 mm deeper. Joint widths, necks, walls and 45-degree flanks scale from the standard 60 mm profile. At the standard 60/13 settings, a tile with tabs on its east and north edges is `(60*nx+6, 60*ny+6, 13)`, with 6 mm and 6.1 mm pocket depths.

`joint_style="original"` is the default. Male ledges reach `t-3`, and female pocket ceilings reach `t-2.8`; their 0.2 mm difference stays 0.2 mm at any size. Tile-facing edge and corner accessories use the same unit size, thickness and joint style as the tile.

### Solid bottom

`Interface.solid_bottom_mm` (`--solid-bottom-thickness-mm`, manifest key `solid_bottom_mm`) adds a continuous floor of thickness `s` below the normal body. It is off at 0, and the key is left out of identities and manifests then, so existing names and output don't change. Z=0 is still the underside and the southwest body corner is still the XY datum. The body is `t+s` thick, with the R1 underside round at Z=0 and the 3 mm socket entry round at the top. X sockets and round holes are cut from the top down to Z=`s`, so they keep their depth `t` and end in a closed floor. Plug projection stays `t-0.2`, which leaves a seated plug tip 0.2 mm above that floor, and rod insertion stays `t-1`.

Original tile-edge joints run through the floor: male ledges reach `t+s-3` and female ceilings `t+s-2.8`, so parts with a solid bottom still assemble by lowering the female side onto the male tab. Female pockets therefore stay open at the underside; the neighbour's male tab, whose own half-hole has a floor, fills them. Tiles, layout fillers, edge and corner pieces (including their edge holes) and ramps get the floor; ramps rise `t+s` over the unchanged 50 mm run. X-plug accessories, rods, braces and support rails are unchanged, reject a nonzero value when made on their own, and use the normal interface in catalogues. Solid-bottom parts only join tile edges with the same `s`. The Zeekr extras treat their 40 mm edges and rear-panel contour pieces like edges and corners: the measured outline is unchanged, the body is `t+s` thick, and joints, sockets and holes follow the rules above, so the pieces sit `s` higher than in the test fit. A solid bottom can't be combined with full-height joints or stacks. Roof enforcers follow the raised ceiling at `t+s-2.8`.

The X socket scales across its face with unit size, from the standard 44.556 mm throat. The plug scales from the same shape, while the small plug/socket differences, `fit_offset` and the 0.08 mm bracket-post inset stay the same in millimetres. Plug projection is `t-0.2`; the 2 mm round on the plug tip and the 3 mm round at the socket entry also keep their size. On an upright bracket post, the scaled face lies in X/Z and tile thickness sets its insertion depth along Y.

The standard settings are 60 mm units, 13 mm thickness and zero fit offset. Custom parts match other parts made with the same settings; they aren't meant to mix with standard 60/13 parts. Unit size must be at least 30 mm and thickness at least 6 mm. Cell counts, copy counts, build dimensions, packing gaps, the default 10 mm holes, stop heights, the 50 mm ramp run and slicer settings don't scale. Edge rounds keep their size too. The support-rail dovetail is a separate fixed interface and doesn't scale with the tile grid.

## Open-through tile-edge joints

`joint_style="full-height"` is an alternate tile-edge joint. It extends the male tabs to the top and opens the female pockets through the roof. It isn't related to cargo-stop height, tile thickness or printer build height, and its male tabs don't fit original roofed female pockets.

At standard 60/13 dimensions, the negative-X pocket comes very close to the neighbouring X-socket entry. The remaining web is about 0.146 mm thick at Z=12.5 and opens to the outside near Z=12.75, even on a no-hole tile. The CAD can still be a valid solid, but that doesn't make the web strong enough to print or use. This joint style stays experimental.

## Exact footprints and hole scopes

Exact layouts use whole cells at the selected unit size and add the leftover width or depth to the tiles on the outside edges, which have no joints there. Filler placement can be balanced or biased to the positive or negative sides, but it doesn't change unit spacing or interface size. Assembly frames describe the floor; print packing and print poses are separate.

The full 10 mm round-hole pattern is the default. `full` scope tries every half-unit grid point except the X-socket centres, including points on the tile's edges and corners, where the hole is shared with the neighbouring part. `interior` keeps only whole holes inside the tile, and `hole_diameter=None` or `--no-holes` makes a tile without holes. At standard 60 mm units, 1x1, 2x1, 2x3 and 4x4 tiles get 8, 13, 29 and 65 holes. Hole diameter stays 10 mm when unit size changes. A position is skipped when there isn't room next to a socket, joint or neighbouring hole, or when it falls on an outside edge with filler; the manifest records each position and why. If none fit, try a smaller hole or use `--no-holes`.

A valid solid with holes says nothing about strength or bridge quality. Check the hole positions, the material left between them and the sliced paths for your settings.

### Perimeter edge projections and completed holes

`edge-x`, `edge-y`, `corner-in` and `corner-out` keep the same joint positions at every width; only the free body changes as it extends 10, 20 or 30 mm outward. By default they carry the other half of the tile's edge holes at every width; `--plain-edge` leaves them out. Faces that meet a tile or a neighbouring edge or corner use the tile's 1 mm rounds, and the free outward outline keeps 3 mm rounds; the shared joint shapes are added afterwards and stay unchanged.

Only straight `edge-x`/`edge-y` parts also accept `edge_outward=40`, with original roofed joints. The 40 mm is physical body width from the tile boundary, independent of unit size and excluding male tabs; at 60/13 the overall sizes are `60*nx x 46 x 13` for male and `60*nx x 40 x 13` for female. Custom units scale length and joint profiles, thickness sets height, and the 3 mm rounds and hole diameter keep their size. There are no 40 mm corners or full-height versions.

Catalogue edges and corners use the same hole size and the same edge positions as their tiles. With the default full 10 mm pattern, 10, 20 and 30 mm pieces carry the other half of each edge hole; with `--no-holes` or interior-only holes they are plain at all three widths. Custom hole diameters don't scale with unit size, and the edge pieces use the same diameter. A tile edge supplies one half of each hole and the edge piece the other; at a tile corner the neighbouring edge and corner pieces supply the remaining quarters. Edge pieces don't add an outer row of holes or fill an existing one.

If no edge hole of the chosen size fits the interface, an automatically matched piece is made plain; with `--complete-edge-holes` you get an error instead of a moved hole or a cut into a socket or joint. At 10 mm outward, a typical piece with holes has 2 mm of material from an end hole to the rounded outside, and faces as close as about 0.08--0.1 mm around some joint and bearing features. Straight 10 mm edges with 10 mm holes have printed successfully for one user; printer, material, load and measurements weren't recorded, and that doesn't cover corners or set a strength limit.

Outer corners made of one male and one female half (v1+v2 and v5+v4) are rounded as the same full L as whole variants v3 and v6, then split at the diagonal seam before each half gets its tile joint. The seam is hidden when assembled; it has no gap and no clip, and the outside keeps the same rounded L.

## Non-printing roof modifiers

Roof support is off by default. Painted roof support works with original-style `part` and `layout` jobs, including jobs that mix tiles and accessories on one plate. It targets the downward-facing ceiling over every female pocket: tile west and south edges, female `edge-y` strips, the female sides of `corner-in` and `corner-out` pieces, female ramps and the female Zeekr contour caps. Pocket positions come from the same joint plans the geometry is built from, and each roof is named by the side its pocket opens toward (`west`, `south`, `north` or `east`) and numbered along that side. Male edges and designs without female pockets get no enforcers; a job without any female roof is rejected. Painted support can't be used with catalogues, extras, stacks, full-height joints or models that Bambu export turns over (plates, brackets, stops, angled stops, rods and rail ends).

`critical` coverage uses two nominal 3 mm bands per roof. It stays at least 6 mm from the roof centre and at least 1 mm outside any edge hole there. `full` keeps the whole roof footprint. A south edge hole can split one roof into two faces, so a standard full-hole 2x1 tile has three roofs, six critical modifiers or five full modifiers.

The enforcer half-span is `min(max(1.0, generation_layer_height), roof_thickness)`, giving Z=9.2--11.2 around the default Z=10.2 ceiling; a solid bottom raises both by its thickness. This spans multiple potential layer planes; it is not a permanent 2 mm support slab. Bambu's manual-support calculation selects current-layer regions inside the enforcer and removes regions already covered by the preceding model layer. Changing a profile does not regenerate the masks, so actual contacts still need checking.

Native `support_enforcer` parts aren't tile solids. STEP exports and model packing bounds leave them out, while Bambu print transforms move them with the tile. The slicer can grow support beyond the mask and into the west and south edge holes. On a standard full-hole 2x1 tile, support fills the three holes at (0,30), (30,0) and (90,0) while printing. Remove it through the open underside and female edges before assembly. Edge holes on other parts fill the same way wherever one meets a targeted roof, which is at each female joint centre: (30,0) per cell on a female `edge-y` strip, (0,30) and (30,0) on corner-in v4, (60,30) and (30,60) on corner-out v3, and (0,210) and (0,270) on the east-north Zeekr cap, for example. The manifest lists them for each non-tile target as `support_may_occupy_openings`. Female ramps have no holes under their roofs. A solid bottom doesn't close these openings, because the pocket itself runs through the floor. A female ramp keeps its own normal Auto support in a roof job, so the job's PETG/PLA zero-contact settings apply to that support too.

PETG for the model and support base is slot 1, and PLA for the interface is slot 2. The PETG/PLA workflow sets `support_top_z_distance=0`, `independent_support_layer_height=0`, `support_interface_spacing=0`, `support_interface_top_layers=2` and `support_on_build_plate_only=0`. These five keys are marked as process overrides, so choosing another profile doesn't quietly replace them. Zero contact needs at least two dense layers; a larger count you set is kept. A positive top gap selects a separate gapped request without forcing synchronized layer height.

This zero-gap setup is only for a suitable PETG model/support base with a PLA contact interface. Same-material and unsupported pairs are rejected. If you swap materials in the slicer, revisit the contact settings instead of leaving zero gap on materials that may fuse. The workflow keeps `support_object_xy_distance=0.4`; in a standard 2x1 test slice, moving from 0.35 to 0.40 mm removed small side-wall overlaps without changing the first-layer footprint. It doesn't change bridge, cooling, speed, prime, flush or automatic-foot settings.

### Auto roof support

`RoofSupportSettings(mode="auto")` (`--roof-support-mode auto`) replaces the painted enforcers with object-scoped `normal(auto)` support. Global `enable_support` stays 0, while the PETG/PLA contact keys stay as in painted mode: support filament 1, interface filament 2, top gap 0, at least two dense interface layers, independent layers off, build-plate-only off and 0.4 mm XY distance. An object gets support when it has a female pocket roof (found as above) or when its family already uses normal Auto support on its own: normal stops, shallow brackets and rods. All supports on those objects then use the PLA interface. Female ramps qualify both ways. Auto mode works with part, layout, catalogue and extras jobs and with models that Bambu export turns over, but not with stacks or full-height joints. Painted mode is part/layout only; `roof_support.ROOF_SUPPORT_JOB_KINDS` holds that table for both the CLI and export checks.

PLA plates need a prime tower. CargoGrid uses Bambu's default tower and writes no tower settings; each plate only gets `wipe_tower_x`/`wipe_tower_y`, and models keep clear of a reserved area around that position. When Bambu Studio 02.08.02.61 opens a project it checks every stored tower position against its own size estimate (`PartPlate::estimate_wipe_tower_size`): the estimated square must sit at least 15 mm inside the area every nozzle reaches, which on the H2D is X 25..325 and Y 0..320. A position outside that range is moved, so a tower written closer to the edge ends up nearer the parts than planned. `plates.BambuTowerEstimate` repeats the estimate: an 8 mm rib allowance plus the square root of 1.5 times the purge volume (30 mm³ per filament and 10 mm of 1.75 mm filament per nozzle change) divided by the layer height, but at least Bambu's minimum depth for the tower height (5 mm at 5 mm tall, rising to 20 mm at 100 mm). It reproduces the positions Bambu Studio wrote into saved projects: a 25.5 mm square and X 284.494 at 0.32 mm layers, and 30.8 mm and X 279.235 at 0.2 mm layers. At the 0.4 mm nozzle's 0.24 mm layers it is 28.6 mm. Each plate's tower therefore goes at Y 15 and X = 325 - 15 - the square for its group's tallest part, rounded down to 0.01 mm, where Bambu leaves it. The reserved area is that square plus Bambu's automatic brim (8 mm at 100 mm tall and above, less below) on every side, 1 mm more behind and out to X 325: local slices put the real tower up to 0.6 mm behind its estimated square, and the nozzle-change purge reaches X 325. Models stay 6.5 mm from it, because Bambu's automatic support-foot expansion grows the first support layer up to about 5 mm past each part. On the tower's left side the gap is 1.5 mm, which keeps the 246 mm-wide tiles with a 5-cell side on tower plates. That gap is checked rather than derived: every plate of the published projects passed Bambu's path-conflict check with it at 0.32 mm (0.8 nozzle) and 0.24 mm (0.4 nozzle) layers, but at 0.2 mm layers the larger tower conflicted with a 5x4 tile rotated so its female edge and support faced the tower, and with a female edge strip beside it. That is why the 0.4 profile uses 0.24 mm layers. Bambu's check also ignores the nozzle-change wipe line, which can start up to 7.5 mm left of the tower and pass over the edge of such a tile. The same foot expansion caused path conflicts between nested edges at 4 mm and put a foot 0.03 mm past the front of the bed, so plates with supported parts also use 8 mm model clearance and a 6 mm front/back plate inset. With these reservations the H2D catalogue needs 26 plates at both nozzle sizes, one more than without auto support. In Bambu Studio 02.08.02.61 CLI slices every plate of both catalogues and both Zeekr sets, at both nozzle sizes, sliced with PETG on either nozzle, including the 120 mm bracket plate that the earlier X 290.5, Y 8 position pushed past X 325 when PETG was on the left. In the GUI a path conflict is only a warning on the plate being previewed, while MakerWorld re-slices uploads and refuses those plates, so `tools/makerworld_exports.py --bambu-studio` slices every plate of the published projects with the Bambu Studio CLI before writing them. The plain catalogue puts the measured `packing.PrimeTower` reach in the front-left corner of its usable area, at least 15 mm from the bed edges. Outline-nested edge groups treat the reserved area as an obstacle that no part's bounding box may enter, the same rule rectangle packing uses. `cargo_grid.plates.plan_plates` does this planning for the catalogues and the Zeekr set: callers pass ordered, titled groups with their own gap, and a group with any supported design gets a tower reservation for its base area and the 4 mm foot allowance. The manifest records the mode, every supported object with its reason and roofs, the openings support may occupy, and each plate's tower origin, reserved bounds and model clearance per side.

### Slice mode and first-layer defaults

Every generated Bambu plate starts in `Auto For Match` / Convenience Mode without a saved physical nozzle map. `--roof-nozzle-slots` switches a roof-support job to Custom mapping. In Bambu's native values, `Auto For Flush` means Filament-Saving Mode and `Manual` means Custom filament grouping. The separate `normal(manual)` support type selects enforcer-defined support and doesn't switch filament grouping.

The zero-contact workflow sets `support_on_build_plate_only` to false. Initial support-foot expansion is left to Bambu unless `--roof-foot-expansion-mm` is supplied; `0` means zero expansion. Automatic nozzle choices can change with the loaded project and filaments, so check them before slicing.

Expansion changes the first support/raft layer, not the model CAD. It may affect bed contact, clearance and removal even when roof-facing interface coverage is unchanged. Neither zero nor automatic expansion is universally correct. Check the actual support and model extrusion footprints, including declared widths and arcs, and distinguish those from nominal CAD sections at the same Z. Layer discretization can make those representations differ.

## Stacks and accessory scope

Stacking separates copies of the same tile with throwaway support and release layers. The gap must leave some support-base thickness after both interfaces. The manifest records quantities, partial batches and material roles. A valid stack file still doesn't tell you how cleanly the parts will separate.

The accessory catalogue contains independently authored functional edge/corner pieces, X-plug plates, normal full-solid stops, angled stops, vertical tile brackets, round-hole rods, upper rod braces and physical support rails. It isn't a copy of another design's contours or extra mechanisms. Support rails join end to end with their own dovetails and stand 25 mm deep; they don't use X plugs, and nothing latches the mat to a rail.

What the catalogue includes depends on the build volume and interface settings. Oversized parts are listed as omitted rather than shrunk, and ordered tile sizes stay distinct even when either orientation fits. Bambu uses the per-family poses listed under [Export checks](#export-checks), then packing may add a 90-degree XY turn.

### Round-hole rods and upper braces

`Rod(above_mat_height_mm=120, peg_diameter_mm=10, tile_thickness_mm=13)` and `RodBrace(center_spacing_mm=60, bore_diameter_mm=10)` are separate small parameter types, with families `rod` and `rod-brace`. They do not have an `Interface`, cell counts or tile/X joints. `make_rod` / `make_rod_brace` generate the solids; `rod_datums` / `brace_datums` describe their mating dimensions. The shared accessory dispatch and catalogue accept these types without adding round-part defaults to existing accessory identities.

Rod height is measured above the mat's seating plane, including the Ø18 x 3.2 mm stop collar. The shaft stays Ø10 mm with an R2 top. Insertion depth is tile thickness minus 1 mm: the standard 13 mm tile gives a 12 mm peg, comprising 11 mm straight engagement and a 1 mm lead. Source orientation is upright along Z, peg tip at Z=0 and collar underside at Z=12. Standard 120/240 mm rods therefore measure 132/252 mm overall. Custom tile thickness changes only insertion depth; unit size doesn't scale the peg or shaft diameter, collar, height or rounds.

Braces have physical centre spacings of 60 or 120 mm and overall bounds 78/138 x 18 x 6.4 mm. Their capsule ends are R9, top edges R1 and bottom chamfer 0.4 mm. Each bore has 5.6 mm straight depth plus a 0.4 mm lead at each mouth. Size and bore labels are part of the solid. Both catalogue braces use Ø10 mm bores for the Ø10 mm rod shafts. Neither spacing nor bore diameter scales with unit size.

The standard bore and shaft have the same nominal 10 mm diameter. For a custom `bore_diameter_mm`, diametral clearance is the bore diameter minus shaft diameter; radial clearance is half that difference. This parameter describes customization, not additional catalogue variants, and nominal CAD clearance is not an as-printed measurement. For standard tiles, a 60 mm pair is (30,60) to (90,60) within one 2x2 tile. A 120 mm pair is (60,30) to (180,30) across two ordinary 2x1 tiles, with the second body origin translated 120 mm along X. These are complete round holes checked in the generated tile solids, not X openings or exposed half-holes. Check the chosen holes on a custom mat rather than assuming every half-unit grid site is usable.

Bambu exports apply Y=+90 degrees to rods before packing and scope normal Auto support to rod objects. STEP, STL and the plain 3MF keep the upright model pose. Braces retain their flat source pose with bores along Z and no object support. The H2D catalogue puts the two rods and two braces on `Rods and upper braces`. Custom dimensions and fit remain untested.

### Vertical tile brackets

`vertical-tile-bracket` supports three depth-matched configurations—floor 1x2 -> wall 1x2, floor 2x1 -> wall 2x1 and floor 2x2 -> wall 2x2—and two independent-height configurations—floor 1x1 -> wall 1x2 and floor 2x1 -> wall 2x2. API `nx` is base/wall X width, `ny` is floor-base Y depth, and optional `panel_height_cells` is wall Z height; leaving it out, or setting it equal to `ny`, gives the original bracket and name. Floor and wall posts use the same unit size and thickness. (The old `lock-90` name is rejected with a pointer to this family.)

The bracket is one full-width, solid-backed wedge. In general, a floor base is `nx*u` wide by `ny*u` deep, and its downward plug tips reach Z=`-(t-0.2)`. At standard 60/13 dimensions, the original bases are 60x120, 120x60 and 120x120 mm, plug tips are Z=-12.8, and the lower body through Z=3.1 is unchanged. There are no outboard tabs, through channels or split parts.

For base depth `d`, the wall-tile seat is Y=`d-t`, the post tips end at Y=`d-0.2`, and wall rows are spaced by `u`. Panel bottom and the bearing plane stay at the fixed physical Z=6.1 mm. A full-width strip from Z=4.1 to 6.1 provides nominal zero-gap bearing. At standard 60/13 dimensions with the illustrated full-hole tiles, planar contact is about 260.892 mm2 on 1x2 and 521.784 mm2 on the two-column brackets. Those figures describe CAD contact, not insertion force or load capacity.

The wall tile remains a separate part. The default placement has its underside facing out. For the top face outward, rotate it Z=180 then X=-90; use that same orientation on every adjoining wall tile because left and right swap. The backing closes off the interior holes it covers while assembled. At standard thickness those pockets are about 13 mm deep; edge cutouts aren't all sealed bores. Same-orientation left, right and upper neighbours keep their mating positions, while a lower neighbour is blocked by the floor bracket.

The bracket itself doesn't take a solid bottom. A solid-bottom wall tile works in the default underside-outward placement, where its extra floor lies beyond Y=`d`. The top-outward placement pushes the posts in from the tile's underside, which a solid bottom closes, so use a tile without one there.

All five brackets use the same wall post, which works in both wall orientations. It has no flare at its base, its straight part is 0.08 mm smaller than the socket, and a 0.1 mm transition leads to the usual 2 mm round tip. For thickness `t`, that transition spans insertion depths `t-2.3` to `t-2.2`; at standard 13 mm thickness it is 10.7--10.8 mm. The post extends 2 mm behind the seat for support. In the standard 1x1-floor to 1x2-wall CAD check, the top-outward tile seats with zero volume collision, while the underside-outward tile keeps 5.439166 mm3 total nominal overlap. A straight top-outward insertion sweep reaches the same overlap as the tip crosses the narrow entry. Insertion force and retention still need a print test.

The two shallow variants retain a 60 mm floor depth, panel seat Y=47, panel plug tips Y=59.8 and the original two-row wall Z grid at standard dimensions. Their natural filled rear profile rises 107 mm across the available 42.8 mm upper run rather than forcing the original 45-degree slope. The upper rear body stays behind the connector root while both floor and panel X regions remain exact. All five brackets use R3 at the analogous exposed thick front-to-slope transition; other thick free body edges remain R2, the thin bearing lip remains R1 and the internal panel-bearing corner stays exact. Standard shallow bounds remain 60x60x140.378 mm and 120x60x140.378 mm including downward plugs.

Shallow Bambu exports use Y=-90 degrees broad-side-down and object-scoped normal Auto. At official H2D 0.8/0.32 and 0.4/0.20 PETG settings, both variants had one connected model first layer, complete model-layer schedules and paths inside shared reach. With support off, the slicer warned about floating cantilevers. Auto generated about 6.74/3.93 g support for the one-column variant and 19.91/14.06 g for the two-column variant, reaching both floor and panel X mating regions. Those regions can be reached in the side-down pose, but support removal, fit, stability and strength haven't been checked on a print.

### Adjustable native-CAD trunk blocker

The trunk blocker is deliberately outside `Accessory`, `accessory_variants()` and every standard/H2D catalogue. It is a normal `extras trunk-blocker` recipe built entirely from native Cargo-Grid BREP source; the Python API retains the source-oriented STEP/STL/core-3MF export without adding a separate top-level CLI command. The three-part mechanism contains the 120x60 mm analytic base, two identical underside X anchors, twin racks, two plain centre-guide walls, a flat passage floor, four locally reinforced blind pilots, a solid-underside 18 mm keeper, a 120x60x8 mm wall, three 117.5 mm fingers, triangular roots, matched-ramp locking teeth, 14 mm-high tooth carriers, three 24 mm push pads and two shared bidirectional front panel posts. This does not change existing tile, bracket, stop, ramp or catalogue defaults.

The fixed base underside is Z=0 and its two-cell body occupies Y=10..130 mm. Its original analytic underbody anchor remains centred at X=0/Y=100 mm, and an identical copy is centred at X=0/Y=40 mm so the pair follows the standard 60 mm Cargo-Grid pitch. Both project 12.8 mm below the base and retain the same profile, direction and R2 tip/root treatment. The moving wall's local rear face stays at Y=0 while the 8 mm backing extends toward negative Y; its fingers point in positive Y, and the static assembly transform moves the pusher by Y=`6.5-extension` mm and Z=5.35 mm. That compensating Y offset moves the wall and reinforcement toward the unchanged world-space rack/tooth stations while the carrier, teeth, pads, tabs and fingertips move 6.5 mm toward the wall in pusher-local coordinates. The finger bodies run from local Z=-0.25 to 11.40, or world Z=5.10 to 16.75. The wall backing, reinforcement roots, moving-tooth carriers and moving teeth extend down to that same local -0.25 mm bed-facing datum, while their tops and working faces remain in place; the reinforcement keeps its 11.35 mm join datum except for the local union and R1 transition required by the higher finger surface. The two front connector centres remain 30 and 90 mm above the pusher floor. Each front connector uses `accessories.make_bidirectional_panel_connector()`, the same 0.08 mm inset stem, 0.1 mm transition and unchanged R2 tip used by vertical tile brackets. The native post projects 12.8 mm outward and extends 2 mm inside the backing. Native checks cover ordinary wall-tile insertion with either the top or underside facing outward, but do not establish printed insertion force or retention.

Composition applies two rigid transforms to the existing bidirectional post and fuses each hidden root into the backing. Verification compares each exposed front post against the same shared BREP in its transformed frame and requires less than 0.000001 mm3 symmetric difference. Material at least 0.00001 mm behind the front mounting plane must have less than 0.000001 mm3 difference from the connector-free backing/mechanism, the pusher must remain one valid connected positive-volume solid, exactly three manufactured solids must round-trip through STEP and three closed oriented meshes must round-trip through core 3MF. Pairwise part overlap must remain below 0.000001 mm3.

Edge treatment follows the accessory-family convention of coupled, feature-specific BREP fillets rather than a blanket post-process. The base outer perimeter, wall envelope and three reinforcement diagonal ridges use R2. Each finger's longitudinal and tip edges, all squeeze-pad edges, the six reinforcement-to-wall/finger transitions, the keeper top front/rear edges and its four outer vertical edges use R1. Filleting fingers before restoring the teeth and carriers, and filleting the keeper before cutting screw geometry, preserves those working faces. A transverse section through the free finger spans measures 103.991592654 mm2 for each nominal 9x11.65 mm outer finger and 115.641592654 mm2 for the nominal 10x11.65 mm centre finger; these are geometric areas, not strength ratings.

The analytic underbody anchor profiles; shared native front X profile, projection and root; rack and moving-tooth ramps and locking faces; tooth-carrier keeper-contact faces; guide, flat floor and keeper bearing surfaces; and all pilot, bore, seat and countersink surfaces remain unrounded functional geometry. Rack and moving ramps derive from one radial/axial ratio of 4/3, measured as 53.130102 degrees on the resulting BREP faces. The test geometry uses 8 mm pitch: each fixed tooth is 4.2 mm deep with a 3.15 mm ramp and 2 mm tip land, while each moving tooth is 6 mm deep with a 4.5 mm ramp and the same 2 mm land. Both retain their 14 mm top datum and are backed by local carriers outside the modeled 24..89.5 mm flex transition; the moving teeth and carriers extend 0.25 mm farther down only to meet the common bed plane. Each outer-finger centre is X=+/-15.8 mm, its relaxed body reaches X=+/-20.3 mm and its tooth tip reaches X=+/-23.8 mm. The fixed tips are X=+/-20.8 mm, leaving 3 mm nominal tooth engagement. The locking faces retain 0.2 mm axial backlash and their unchanged planes meet over 42.75 mm2 per tooth; this area is per tooth and does not imply equal three-tooth loading. The 3.7 mm release leaves 0.7 mm nominal radial tip clearance. The two uncapped centre-guide walls occupy X=5.10..7.45 mm and X=-7.45..-5.10 mm, Y=13.5..48 mm and Z=4.9..16.85 mm. Their 10.20 mm channel leaves 0.10 mm nominal clearance on each side of the 10 mm centre finger, while the unchanged outer faces retain 0.15 mm for each fully released outer finger. ### Floor ramps

`ramp` is an independently authored floor-to-mat transition with original roofed tile-edge joins. Its width is an integer number of unit cells, its rise is tile thickness `t`, and its finished body run stays 50 mm in positive Y. API `ramp_join="female"` and CLI `--ramp-join female` are the defaults, and female ramps keep their names. Each cell has one female pocket at its half-unit centre, receiving a tile's north male edge at Y=0.

`ramp_join="male"` / `--ramp-join male` uses the same wedge with shared male tile-edge tabs pointing toward negative Y. Tabs project `0.1*u` beyond the joining face, so the source bounds are `nx*u` by `50+0.1*u` by `t`: 60x56x13 mm for a standard one-cell male ramp. This extra tab projection is not taken from the slope's 50 mm run. The manifest's `mating_datums` separates body run, tab projection and overall depth. Only the outward half of each shared male tool is added; its construction wall cannot flatten the slope at larger unit sizes.

For a matching tile whose body begins at (0,0), place a female ramp along the north edge with translation `(0, tile_depth, 0)`, or along the east edge with Z=-90 rotation followed by translation `(tile_width, ramp_width, 0)`. Place a male ramp along the south edge with Z=180 rotation followed by translation `(ramp_width, 0, 0)`, or along the west edge with Z=90 rotation and no translation. These placements start at the tile corner; offset by whole cells for a longer mat. Rotation changes the direction the slope faces, not whether the joints are male or female. At standard dimensions the male tabs project 6 mm into the south's 6 mm pocket or the west's 6.1 mm pocket, and the ledge/roof seating gap stays 0.2 mm.

The profile is one filled wedge with a 10 mm flat shelf before the shelf-to-slope round and a rounded nose that meets the floor. Side and nose rounds are R2, the underside edge at the joining face is R1, and shared tile-joint tools provide the joins. At standard 60/13 dimensions the female ramp pocket is 6.1 mm deep. Separate ramps meet without overlap, while a multi-cell ramp is one continuous solid. The high joining rim is left unchanged.

The flat carrier meets the slope through a tangent R32 arc. At standard thickness the bend is about 13.246 degrees: it starts at Y=6.284 mm, ends at Y=13.617 mm and spans 7.332 mm horizontally while dropping 0.851 mm. The main slope plane, 50 mm finished body run and 13 mm rise remain unchanged. The broader curve replaces the much subtler R2 shelf blend; it does not round the high joining rim. Since the standard female pocket ends at Y=6.1 mm, its 2.8 mm roof remains intact beneath the flat shelf.

R32 is a physical maximum, not a unit-scaled radius. For slope angle `a`, the resolved radius is `min(32, (10-2)/tan(a/2))`, retaining at least 2 mm of high flat shelf when thicker custom ramps make the slope steeper. For example, a 60 mm-thick ramp uses about R15.330. The manifest records the resolved shelf radius. Custom unit sizes or thicknesses can place some pocket roof beneath the curved top; their local roof thickness is not necessarily the standard 2.8 mm. The shelf and nose arcs are constructed in section before extrusion and side rounding, so shallow slopes cannot silently lose their blend. Finished-surface checks measure radius and tangent continuity at both boundaries across the width. A CAD viewer may still draw those tangent boundaries.

Ramps require original roofed joints. Width and the joins follow unit size, rise follows tile thickness and the finished body run remains the approved physical 50 mm. Fit allowances and rounds keep their size in millimetres. Full-height ramp joints are rejected because a generic cutter applied after rounding left real overlap with the matching tile. There are no corner ramps, separate male adapters, X plugs, holes or added mechanisms.

The source orientation already places the underside on Z=0. Bambu exports turn on normal Auto support for female ramp objects, for their pocket roofs, without enabling global support; male ramps get no support. Packing uses the actual bounds including male tabs. Bambu Studio slices of 1-cell and 5-cell female ramps with the H2D PETG profiles produced one connected first model layer and continuous model schedules. The deposited first layer reached about 48.72 mm of the 50 mm run at 0.8/0.32 and 48.47 mm at 0.4/0.20; the remaining nose is the upward-curving tangent tip. Auto support occupied only the exposed female-pocket region and must be removed before assembly. These are slicer results, not tests of fit, adhesion or load.

With the broad R32 shelf, Bambu Studio 02.08.02.61 slices used the installed H2D 0.8 nozzle / 0.32 mm Balanced Strength / PETG Basic settings on one- and five-cell ramps, plus a one-cell 0.4/0.20 comparison. All model layers were present, and deposited model/support bounds stayed inside common reach; neither brim nor tower was generated. Female ramps retained a connected first model layer and pocket-roof support, with top interface paths at Z=10.12 and Z=10.0 respectively. Male OFF and Auto controls both generated no support, but their first model layer had separate body/tab islands: two for one cell and six for five cells. Auto does not join those islands or establish adhesion. Reopening the projects in Bambu Studio kept the embedded settings, model parts, placements, material assignments and per-object support. The slicer still printed its usual tool-change (T-command) diagnostics; nothing was sent to a printer. Bed adhesion, support removal and printed fit still need a physical trial.

### Normal full-solid vertical stops

`vertical-stop` is a cargo stop, not a wall-tile bracket. The catalogue includes 1x1, 1x2, 2x1 and 2x2 bases at physical shoulder heights H60 and H120 mm. The first count is base X width, the second is base Y depth, and height is measured from the attachment shoulder at Z=0. Unit size changes the base spacing and X profiles; stop height stays 60 or 120 mm. Plug tips are Z=`-(t-0.2)`, or -12.8 mm at standard thickness.

The body is one full-width filled triangular wedge from the 4.1 mm front toe to a solid cargo face at positive Y. It has no open central bay, separate side ribs, wall holes, panel connectors, ledge or tile. The 2x1/H120 geometry is the natural wedge selected for the family rather than an extra-material raised 45-degree toe. A solid CAD body does not request 100% slicer infill.

The slope is solved so that, after the 2 mm rounds, the stop still reaches exactly 60 or 120 mm and the cargo face stays at its exact Y position. One fillet operation rounds all twelve free source edges: cargo cap/perimeter, both diagonal rear boundaries, front/toe perimeter and the complete underside outer perimeter. The X plugs and their 1 mm root rounds don't change at all. R2 on both horizontal boundaries of the 4.1 mm toe leaves a 0.1 mm planar center land at the extreme front; source bodies, STEP roundtrips and closed meshes remain valid.

Each Bambu export works out its X rotation from the actual depth, height and rounded slope, so the broad rear face is down before fit checks and packing. Normal Auto support is set on the stop object only. In Bambu Studio slices with the H2D PETG profiles, 1x1/H120 and 2x1/H120 generated mounting-region support at both 0.8/0.32 and 0.4/0.20; check the sliced paths and plan for removing that support. Other profiles can make different support decisions.

### Accessory edge rounds

Original-style edge and corner bodies are rounded in one coupled operation before the unchanged tile-joint tools are applied. Every edge of a mating face gets the tile's R1: the tile-facing face and the strip or corner ends that butt against neighbouring perimeter pieces on cell lines, including their vertical corners, top and underside. Edges that belong only to the free outward outline keep R3, so the outward top and underside and any inside corner stay comfortable to handle. Outer-corner halves are rounded as the whole L and then split, so their diagonal seam stays sharp. An assembled edge or corner seam then leaves no more of the mat open, seen from above, than two tiles meeting at the same place with the same holes and solid bottom; with a solid bottom and full holes nothing is open through it. Rounding every edge to 3 mm would leave 3-versus-1 mm notches at each piece end, and next to corner holes a gap below the hole floor. With 10 mm corner holes, a 0.1 mm strip of the 1 mm underside round remains between the hole and the joint-tool wall. The Zeekr rear-panel contour caps and ramps have unrounded mating faces, which close as tightly as an R1 seam. Experimental full-height parts keep their own mix of 2 mm and 1 mm rounds.

Straight rail and connector outer bodies use R3, with R3 window corners and R2 window rims. Rail-end variant 1 uses a coupled R0.75 outer body, R2.5 window corners, R1 horizontal window rims and R1.5 sloped underside rims. Variant 2 uses sequential R3 side caps, R0.25 ramp-profile edges, R2/R2 horizontal windows and R1.5 sloped underside rims. Variants 3/4 use coupled R3 bodies with R3/R2 horizontal windows and R1/R0.75 on their sloped underside window rims. These are the largest tested combinations that keep the exact support dovetails and both default/adaptive STEP-volume budgets on macOS and Linux. Small custom units proportionally cap constrained window-rim radii; the separate support dovetail remains fixed. Attachment plates use R2 around the complete 4.1 mm body, then receive exact X plugs and R2 roots.

Angled `lock-45` stops retain a 4.1 mm front base/toe, a 6 mm horizontal cap thickness (about 4.243 mm normal to the 45-degree cargo face), unchanged outer bounds and exact X connectors/roots. One coupled operation applies R2 to all 18 free envelope edges. The 1x1 and 2x2 Bambu poses remain X=-135 degrees; Bambu Studio slices at 0.8/0.32 and 0.4/0.20 generated no support with support off or on Auto.

The 2x2 angled stop supports unit sizes up to 60 mm because its coupled R2 body fails above that span. The 1x1 version is the supported alternative for larger units.

## Export checks

Each exported design must be one valid, positive-volume solid. STEP reimport checks body count, bounds within 0.00001 mm and adaptive BRepGProp volume at 1e-12 against the existing surface-area-times-OCCT-confusion budget. The writer tries OCCT precision modes against those same gates and keeps the first passing file; it doesn't change the source solid or widen a tolerance. The manifest records the chosen mode, delta, method and budget.

An export takes its own copy of the job and shapes. It measures actual source and print-pose bounds before meshing, then reuses those measurements for fit, packing, STEP checks and manifest entries. A 90-degree packing turn permutes the measured bounds; arbitrary print poses are still measured from the rotated CAD. Each STEP precision attempt measures its newly imported solid independently. Nothing is cached on the public `Design`: moving or replacing a shape, changing a pose or editing metadata takes effect on the next export.

Mesh chord tolerance is 0.02 mm. Surface seams are welded only within 0.000001--0.00001 mm, bounded by kernel vertex tolerance and an independent displacement cap. Unmeshed faces are accepted only below 0.0000000001 mm2; all resulting meshes must still be closed, consistently oriented and positive-volume, without zero-area triangles.

A remaining isolated three-edge crack can be closed only when it is an unambiguous oppositely oriented triangle, contains no duplicate face, has maximum edge 0.1 mm, and has area below both 0.0000001 mm2 and its perimeter times OCCT linear confusion. Fifteen barycentric probes, including vertices and edge-quarter points, must lie within OCCT's 0.0000001 mm tolerance of the unchanged CAD surface. No vertices move. Larger, ambiguous, nontriangular or off-surface gaps remain errors. The mesh report records any repair and the strict checks still run afterward.

STEP, STL and 3MF generation share one export lifetime. Each unchanged source solid is tessellated and checked once; STL uses the source coordinates, while 3MF uses rigidly rotated copies of the checked vertex arrays. Triangle winding and connectivity are retained. Separate support and modifier solids are checked in their own right. The arrays are read-only and belong only to that export, so a later bounds query cannot discard the checked result by clearing OCCT's attached triangulation. Source files and 3MF mesh data are written together per design, and arrays are released after their last batch or shared-source use rather than accumulated for the whole catalogue. These checks do not lower mesh resolution or substitute mesh bounds for precise CAD bounds.

Tile construction solves each distinct edge-joint tool once within that tile, then deep-copies the tool before placement and Boolean operations. A location alone is not an ownership boundary: located OCCT shapes can share mutable topology. Standard X-socket bores are cut together before the unchanged entry-rounding operation rather than repeatedly rebuilding the growing tile for each bore. Full-height and custom-unit socket construction keep their existing entry tools.

Manifests record the generator version, resolved parameters, compatibility notes and unsupported combinations. The plain 3MF (without `--bambu`) keeps model orientation and quantities. The Bambu 3MF adds plate, material and modifier metadata. Either file is still unsliced unless you slice it yourself.

Bambu single-part and catalogue exports rotate attachment plates X=180 degrees body-down, rail ends (`support-end`) X=180 degrees bearing-face-down, original vertical tile brackets by their retangented rear-face-down X angle, shallow brackets Y=-90 degrees (broad side down), normal stops by their per-design broad-rear-face-down angle and angled stops X=-135 degrees (back face down) before bounds checks and packing. STEP, STL and the plain 3MF keep model orientation. Recommendations include per-artifact applied flags; Bambu plate items include the exact source-to-project transform, composed with their in-plane packing rotation and translation. Normal-stop and shallow-bracket items carry object-scoped `enable_support=1` and `support_type=normal(auto)` without changing global tile/original-bracket support behavior. The pose is already baked into the Bambu mesh and must not be applied twice. API catalogue callers use `orient_for_bambu=True` for the matching eligibility calculation. Stacks and painted roof support can't include models that Bambu export turns over; auto roof support can.

A rail end's underside is flat for part of its length and then slopes up toward the end, so in model orientation the sloped part hangs over the bed. At Z=0.2 mm only 30-50% of its largest horizontal section touches the bed that way up; turned over onto its flat top bearing face, 82-96% does (variants 1-4: 96, 83, 82 and 82%). In that pose no surface more than about 1 mm above the bed faces down more steeply than 45 degrees; what remains are the bottom-edge rounds, as on a straight rail. The windows and the dovetail run straight through the height, so they print the same either way. Straight rails and connectors have the same bed contact either way up (84% of their largest section) and keep their model orientation. In a Bambu Studio 02.08.02.61 slice with the H2D 0.8 mm nozzle, 0.32 mm Balanced Strength and PETG Basic profiles, each turned-over end puts its whole length on the first layer and prints with no overhang walls and no support; upright, the first layer covered only 45-70 mm of the length and the slope printed as unsupported overhang walls. How flat the ends print and how their dovetails fit still needs a real print.

The H2D dual-safe catalogue is a plan for one machine at the standard 60/13 settings, not a generic build rectangle. Common plates use X25..325, Y0..320 and Z<=320, add a 5 mm model inset and default to at least 4 mm of XY clearance between actual parts. Rectangle-packed plates enforce that clearance conservatively between model bounds. Eligible perimeter groups use all-height projected model footprints only when their concave shapes reduce the plate count, and the prepared export reconstructs those footprints before accepting the placements. Explicit placements must fit the physical build, including its margins, reservations and exclusions, and any narrower per-plate envelope; both checks use exact normal-part bounds, while projected footprints only replace marked inter-part rectangle clearance checks. Edges and corners are grouped by outward width and by whether they carry edge holes, following the tile pattern; empty groups aren't made. Its tile set is the 24 sizes that fit the common area. The 306x306 mm 5x5 doesn't fit the 300 mm common width, so it is listed as omitted rather than given a single-nozzle plate; make it with `part` and choose its nozzle yourself. Every plate keeps automatic Convenience Mode. Model clearance does not include every possible support, brim or tower path, so inspect the sliced project.

Catalogue fit checks measure each generated candidate in the requested source or Bambu pose once. The H2D planner reuses those precise sizes while grouping and packing the same unchanged designs. Sizes are tied to design identity, not names, and live only for that request; later calls measure again so changed shapes or poses cannot reuse stale bounds.

Only the remaining intersection edges are rounded in 3D, in two fixed passes: R6 on the outer corners and grip top solved together with R1.5 on the seat perimeter, then R3 around the hand opening. On handles deeper than 90 mm the flare is shallow enough that a separate seat pass can't blend into the R6 ends of the flare edges, so the two are solved together. These radii must all solve; there are no fallbacks. Both versions build and pass the strict STEP roundtrip at every whole unit size from 30 to 200 mm. Two standard X plugs sit on neighbouring socket centres with the plate's R2 root round. The optional strap bar is 52 x 12 mm in plan, centred across the width with its front face 25 mm in front of the handle's centre line (5 mm behind the front edge at the standard depth), with a 30 x 8 mm slot under an 8 mm bar and R3 top edges.

The Bambu poses come from `pull_handles.print_rotation_x`. The strap-bar handle lies on its front with object-scoped normal Auto support. Its rest face is a common tangent of the grip's R6 top-front edge and the next convex edge below it in the side view: the strap bar's R3 top-front edge, or the R1.5 front seat edge on deeper handles. Of the candidate tangents through the grip, the one tilted furthest toward the top is the hull face. At 60 mm and 30 mm units that is the bar, about X=108.23 degrees; at 45 mm units it is the seat edge, about X=118.32 degrees. The version without the bar turns X=180 onto its flat grip top with no object support. At the standard 60/13 settings, both were the least-support stable resting poses in a slicer comparison of every stable resting face at H2D 0.8 nozzle / 0.32 mm Balanced Strength with Bambu PETG Basic: about 5.0 m of support filament for the strap-bar handle and none for the other. The version without the bar also lies on its front stably, but without object support Bambu Studio warned of a floating cantilever there, and with it the pose needed about 4.0 m of support. Lying on its front, the strap-bar handle's posts take a sideways pull along their layers; the grip-down pose loads the posts across them. Support also reaches the sideways X plugs in the front-down pose. With the H2D 0.4 nozzle, 0.24 mm Standard process and PETG Basic 0.4 profile, the same poses sliced without warnings: about 3.1 m of support for the strap-bar handle and none for the other; the poses weren't compared again there. Other unit sizes use the analogous poses without a slicer comparison. Longer plugs on thicker tiles move the centre of mass, so the strap-bar handle is refused when its centre of mass would sit less than 5 mm inside its two front rest edges, measured across them in the bed plane. That margin is a provisional allowance against tipping while printing, not a measured limit. At 60 and 30 mm units it allows tiles up to about 21.5 mm thick; deeper handles resting on the seat edge keep a much larger margin. The manifest records the margin. The published H2D handle projects come from `tools/makerworld_exports.py`, one PETG-only `Pull-Handle-Set` per H2D nozzle, without roof support or a solid bottom; `--set Pull-Handle-Set` rebuilds only those.

## Optional reference comparison

Portable tests inspect generated solids, dimensions, layout placement, accepted hole locations, STEP reimports, mesh topology, accessory families, separator contacts and 3MF structure without a redistributed reference mesh.

The optional local comparison builds original-style geometry and reads the model IDs and component transforms from a local 3MF you supply. It samples socket rays, directional joining sections, mixed original/new signed intervals and insertion offsets. Near-horizontal mesh intersections use a 0.0001 mm section offset, reported in the output. Its 0.008 mm measurement budget covers tessellation and near-tangent sampling, not a manufacturing allowance.

Sampling is not an exhaustive insertion/collision sweep or full-surface equivalence proof. Rounded upper joining patches may differ even when sampled functional datums match. The reference itself can have both interference and clearance; that is not evidence of intended insertion force, calibration or load capacity. Full-height geometry is never reported as an original-compatible pass.

## What still needs a real print

CAD and slicer checks don't measure insertion force, retention, support release, flatness, heat creep, impact strength or load capacity. Record real results with the exact model revision, material and print profile. No load or restraint rating is supplied.

A test print of the 120/240 mm rods in PETG with a 0.8 mm nozzle and 0.32 mm layers gave a good fit of the 10 mm peg in the mat. That covers the horizontal rod pose and standard 13 mm tiles; diameter, insertion force and filament brand weren't recorded. Test-printed upper braces with the 10.0 mm bore label fit the 10 mm shafts well; which spacing was tested wasn't recorded. Other bore sizes, measured clearances, holding force and load remain untested. Bags rest on the mat; a friction-fit brace doesn't lock the height.

An earlier strap-bar pull handle with a 34 mm hand opening and a 24 mm-thick grip, otherwise the same shape, was printed in the front-down pose with PETG on a 0.8 mm nozzle at 0.32 mm layers. Its X plugs fitted a tile and its support came off cleanly, but the opening was too tight, so the current handle has a 40 mm opening under an 18 mm grip. The current handles, the grip-down pose, insertion force, retention and pull strength haven't been tested.
